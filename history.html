ARCHIVE: TIMELINE

A comprehensive log documenting the evolution of Bring Me The Horizon from a local deathcore act in Sheffield to Grammy-nominated global alternative icons.

Complete Band History

The detailed chronological archive of Bring Me The Horizon's career. This log records their musical evolution, critical studio releases, shifts in lineup, and defining moments across two decades.

2004
Formation & Early Demos
Bring Me The Horizon was formed in March 2004 in Sheffield, South Yorkshire. The founding members—Oliver Sykes (vocals), Lee Malia (guitar), Curtis Ward (guitar), Matt Kean (bass), and Matt Nicholls (drums)—were part of various local bands before uniting. The band's name was famously derived from a line spoken by Captain Jack Sparrow in the film Pirates of the Caribbean: The Curse of the Black Pearl ("Now, bring me that horizon"). They quickly recorded their bedroom demo tape and began playing local shows.
2006 – 2007
Count Your Blessings & Controversy
Following their debut EP This Is What the Edge of Your Seat Was Made For, the band released their first full-length album, Count Your Blessings, in late 2006. The album was deeply rooted in the heavy, chaotic deathcore genre. While it built a massive underground following, it deeply polarized critics and traditional metal fans. Despite the backlash, they won "Best British Newcomer" at the 2006 Kerrang! Awards and embarked on intense touring schedules, including a slot on the Download Festival.
2008 – 2009
Suicide Season & Shifting Sound
The band traveled to Sweden to record with producer Fredrik Nordström. The resulting album, Suicide Season (2008), marked a massive departure from their deathcore roots towards a more structured metalcore sound, incorporating electronic flourishes. During the "Taste of Chaos" tour in 2009, rhythm guitarist Curtis Ward left the band due to exhaustion and failing relationships within the group. He was replaced by Jona Weinhofen (formerly of I Killed the Prom Queen and Bleeding Through).
2010 – 2011
There Is a Hell...
In 2010, the band released their third album with an infamously long title: There Is a Hell Believe Me I've Seen It. There Is a Heaven Let's Keep It a Secret. This album pushed their creative boundaries, adding symphonic, choral, and advanced electronic elements (including programming by a young Sonny Moore, aka Skrillex). The album received widespread critical acclaim and solidified them as leaders of the modern metalcore scene.
2012 – 2014
Sempiternal & The Jordan Fish Era
This era marked the most significant shift in the band's career. Jona Weinhofen left the band, and keyboardist/producer Jordan Fish was officially brought in. Fish's influence completely reshaped their songwriting. Released in 2013 on RCA Records, Sempiternal was a critical and commercial masterpiece, blending heavy riffs with sweeping ambient and electronic textures. Tracks like "Can You Feel My Heart" and "Shadow Moses" became anthems, culminating in a legendary sold-out headline show at London's Wembley Arena in 2014.
2015 – 2017
That's the Spirit & Mainstream Dominance
Opting to self-produce, Sykes and Fish crafted That's the Spirit (2015). The album largely abandoned metalcore in favor of highly polished alternative rock, pop rock, and nu-metal. Supported by massive singles like "Drown" and "Throne", the album debuted at No. 2 on the US Billboard 200 and UK Albums Chart. The band upgraded from clubs to massive arenas globally. In 2016, they performed a landmark charity concert at the Royal Albert Hall accompanied by a full symphony orchestra and choir.
2018 – 2019
amo & Genre-Bending
Continuing their evolution, the band released amo in early 2019. It was their most experimental and divisive record to date, heavily featuring pop, electronic, trap, and even hip-hop influences alongside alternative metal. The risk paid off: amo became their first Number 1 album in the UK and earned them their first Grammy nominations (including Best Rock Song for "MANTRA"). They closed the year as headliners for major international festivals.
2020 – 2022
Survival Horror & Collaborations
During the global lockdown, the band conceptualized the POST HUMAN series—a planned quadrilogy of EPs. The first, POST HUMAN: Survival Horror (2020), was a return to a much heavier sound, mixing metalcore and nu-metal with Doom-inspired synths (co-produced by Mick Gordon). The era was marked by high-profile collaborations, including tracks with BABYMETAL, Yungblud, Nova Twins, and even a heavy remix of "Bad Habits" with Ed Sheeran.
2023 – Present
Departure of Fish & NeX GEn
In late 2023, the band announced the shocking departure of Jordan Fish, who had been instrumental in their sound for over a decade. Continuing as a four-piece (with live touring members), they released the delayed second installment of their series, POST HUMAN: NeX GEn in May 2024. The record shifted towards a deeply nostalgic but futuristic blend of hyperpop, 2000s emo, and post-hardcore, proving the band's ability to constantly reinvent themselves while maintaining their status as titans of modern rock music.
